Gabe & Judy · September 26 – October 13, 2026 · researched Sep 13, Madrid rebuilt Sep 16

One section per stop, in trip order. Each opens with what needs a reservation and when, then what else is worth knowing in that city, then the tapas bars, wine bars and casual rooms that need no booking at all. Madrid

Sep 27 → 30 · 3 nights

La Latina — you are staying on Cava Baja, Madrid's best tapas street. Almost everything below is a walk.

Guinness-certified oldest restaurant in the world, 1725, 300 years marked in 2025. Yes it is heavily touristed; no, it is not fake — the wood-fired ovens are three centuries old and the roasting is the real thing. À la carte only. Order the cochinillo, not the cordero — consensus is clear the suckling pig is the dish. Hemingway ended The Sun Also Rises here. The 13:00 and 23:15 slots are the easiest to get.

Four tables, eight diners a night, inside the world's most famous flamenco tablao, with a 1,200-reference sherry list. Chef David García. The only starred restaurant in your own neighbourhood. Eight seats means it goes first — call, don't just use the form.

⚠ The flamenco is a separate booking — "dinner with show" and "dinner without show" are different products. Settle that on the same call. The only firm price I could find anywhere is a €309 pp package that bundles premium pairings and front-row seats; the €49/€65 figures in circulation predate the star.

The overlap with Faralá: both are star-plus-flamenco. Since the show here is optional, the clean answer is to eat at both and take the show at one. Corral is the definitive grand tablao; Faralá is the Sacromonte register at half the price.

Malacatín

Optional · Mon or Tue LUNCH

Sun–Tue 11:00–18:00 · lunch only
Price
~€25–33

The castizo cocido madrileño — four generations since 1895, unreconstructed, and the cocido is the only main. The most on-brief room in Madrid, but it books weeks ahead and it has to be a lunch, so treat it as a long shot. Taberna de la Daniela (below) is the fallback and needs almost no notice.

malacatin.com

Also worth noting

The field, honestly. Madrid has 31 stars. Dinner availability across your three nights: Sunday 4 · Monday 5–6 · Tuesday 20. Thin at the front of the week, wide open on Tuesday.

  • If Tuesday should be bigger than a one-star — Cebo ★ €160 (16 min walk, the only other walkable one) · Santerra ★ ~€90 · Smoked Room ★★ €180 Hachi (14 seats) · DSTAgE ★★ €195. The two-stars release 120 days out, so they are a cancellation play — ask for the 20:00 seating, which materially improves the odds.
  • Other Monday options — Éter ~€130 (five tables, off most radars) · Clos Madrid ~€95–110 (wine-led) · Saddle ~€190 (classic, formal).
  • Other Sunday options — Clos Madrid · Gofio ★ (Canarian, closed Mon & Tue) · RavioXO ★ ~€70–90.
  • DiverXO is closed Sun–Tue, so it was never on the table. StreetXO (Serrano 47, 3rd floor) is open daily, ~€50–90 pp — the counter is walk-in, no reservations; arrive 15–20 min before doors. RavioXO is the better middle path, since you can actually book it.
  • Michelin at lunch is the workaround for the dead nights: Yugo the Bünker and Quimbaya both do Sunday lunch only; La Tasquería, Clos Madrid, Saddle and Éter all serve Monday lunch.

Cocido madrileño — broth, then chickpeas and vegetables, then the meats. Traditionally a long Thursday lunch. Not a walk-in dish.

  • Taberna de la Daniela Cuchilleros — C/ Cuchilleros 9, 2 min. €31–45. Daily, and the only one of the four that serves cocido at dinner (13–17h & 20–24h). +34 913 662 018. The practical answer.
  • La Bola — C/ de la Bola 5, 16 min. €39.90–47. Individual clay pots over oak charcoal since 1870. Sun–Wed 13:00–16:00 only.
  • Lhardy — Carrera de San Jerónimo 8, 15 min. €50–60+. Madrid's first luxury restaurant, 1839, formal 19th-century room. Sunday 13:00–16:00 only.

Sunday Sep 27 — El Rastro, and the vermut. The flea market runs Sundays 09:00–15:00 on C/ Ribera de Curtidores, five minutes from the hotel. You reach the room around 13:00, so you catch the last ninety minutes — peak crowd, stalls packing down. Treat it as an atmospheric walk-through, not a browsing session. ⚠ It is Madrid's worst spot for pickpockets — front pockets, bags closed and in front. The vermouth hour afterwards is the real ritual, and it happens on your own street.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

  • Bodegas Ricla — C/ Cuchilleros 6, 3 min. Founded 1867, standing room only, vermouth drawn from the barrel, tripe and León cured meats. Sunday 13:30–18:00, under €15. The arrival-day play. Order gildas or boquerones with it — the salt is the point.
  • Taberna La Concha — Cava Baja 7, on your block. The more polished vermouth room; signature "Cocktail Manuela".
  • Taberna Tempranillo — Cava Baja 38. The street's wine bar, 180+ Spanish wines by the glass. Go early, it fills.
  • Casa Lucas — Cava Baja 30. Small, wine-led, lesser-known producers, slow runny tortilla.
  • Casa Lucio — Cava Baja 35. The institution: huevos rotos. Reserve 1–2 weeks ahead for dinner — or use Los Huevos de Lucio at No. 30, the walk-in sibling with the same eggs.
  • Taberna Txakolina — Cava Baja 26. Basque pintxos, salt cod.
  • La Perejila — Cava Baja 25. Chorizo in white wine, vintage and flower-filled, zero pretense.
  • La Venencia — C/ Echegaray 7, Huertas. Sherry only, cash only, no photographs, your tab chalked on the bar. Unchanged since the 1930s. The best single room in Madrid.
  • Angelita Madrid — C/ Reina 4. ~500 bottles, ~70 by the glass, cocktail cellar downstairs. Reserve for the restaurant, walk in for the bar.
  • La Barra de la Tasquería — Estévez's original site reopened as a standing bar; 2026 Bib Gourmand. Same DNA, no menu commitment.

Also worth noting

  • Xerta — downstairs in your own hotel. Ebro Delta cooking, ~90% of ingredients from the delta. Delta menu €80 (7 courses) · Product €115 (9) · seasonal lunch €55 including two glasses. Tue–Sat, closed Sun & Mon. ⚠ It lost its Michelin star — held one 2016–2024, now "Michelin Selected". Two sources agree; not confirmed at Michelin directly. A very good, very low-effort Wed Sep 30 arrival dinner after the train — but it should not take one of your event nights. The hotel also has Kintsugi (Japanese, Thu–Mon) and the rooftop.
  • Disfrutar books up to 12 months ahead (€315 + €170 pairing). Not happening at eleven days — but Compartir Barcelona, from the same three chefs, is the realistic way into that lineage.
  • Other one-stars open your nights — Dos Palillos €140/€175, Asian tapas at a standing counter in Casa Camper, serious sake list (closed Sun & Mon) · Koy Shunka €178/€218, Japanese kappo counter around a wood-fired kitchen (closed Sun & Mon) · COME by Paco Méndez €185, Mexican-Mediterranean with elBulli technique (closed Sat & Sun) · Alkimia €188, contemporary Catalan in the Moritz brewery, 18 seats — but Friday is lunch only, so Wed or Thu only.
  • The easier three-stars, per local guides — ABaC €225–295 ("reservations are easy to get"), Cocina Hermanos Torres €310 (~2 weeks), Lasarte €345 dinner (~2 weeks). All over budget, all more formal than Mont Bar.
  • Bib Gourmands — Cruix (Eixample, 7 courses ~€45, bookable about a week out) is the gettable one. Berbena (Gràcia) is the critics' darling but books many weeks ahead and closes weekends — likely too late.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

  • Bar Mut — Pau Claris 192, 5 min walk. The Eixample wine bar: marble counter, excellent list, foie, market seafood. Gets tight.
  • Entrepanes Díaz — Eixample. The great Barcelona sandwich bar — calamari baguette, suckling pig — white-jacketed waiters, very fast.
  • Paco Meralgo — Muntaner 171. Seafood-forward classic tapas counter, the locals' standby.
  • Quimet & Quimet — Poble Sec. Standing-room-only montaditos built from tinned seafood, since 1914. No reservations — arrive at opening.
  • El Xampanyet — El Born. House cava and anchovies since 1929, tiled and chaotic. Go early.
  • Bar Cañete — Raval. Refined Catalan cooking at a long counter; the best high-end/low-formality combination in the old city. Walk-ins wait ~30 min, or book.
  • Bar La Plata — Gòtic. Four dishes, three wines, under €10, since 1945. Lunch and early evening only.
  • Bodega Quimet — Gràcia. Vermouth bodega since the 1950s, barrels and conservas.
  • La Cova Fumada — Barceloneta. Birthplace of the bomba, no sign on the door, closes ~15:00, no reservations.

Granada

Oct 3 → 6 · 3 nights

Casa 1800, at the foot of the Albaicín by Plaza Nueva. One of the last Spanish cities where a free tapa arrives with every drink — and it improves with each round.

Reserve these

Granada's only Michelin star, on the road that climbs from Plaza Nueva to the Alhambra. Downstairs is a flamenco tablao, and the show is a dinner add-on rather than a separate evening. Andalusian cooking reinterpreted — deconstructed tortilla del Sacromonte, Nazarí-era references, falafel.

Why Saturday: you land from Barcelona at ~14:30 with nothing else scheduled, and it sets the Andalusian tone the night before Alhambra day. Oct 4 is ruled out — sunset at Mirador de San Nicolás collides with a 19:00 curtain.

⚠ No vegetarian menu, and menus cannot be changed after arrival — allergies must be declared at booking. ⚠ Closing day unverified: the site shows service 13:30–23:00 daily but no source confirms a day off. Saturday is near-certain.

Guía Repsol lists different prices (€68 / €84 / €104, and a third menu called Esencia Nazarí) — prices have moved, so confirm at booking. The ground floor is El Quejío Wine Bar, à la carte, if you'd rather drink than commit.

restaurantefarala.com·TheFork·El Quejío wine bar

Also worth noting

  • Atelier – Casa de Comidas — 2026 Bib Gourmand, ~15 min from the centre. Closed Sun & Mon, which leaves only Sat Oct 3 — and that is Faralá night. Probably skip it this trip.
  • Mon Oct 5 is the Guadix day — wineries and lunch out there are in the On the road section. Back for a slow Albaicín evening.
  • Granada is a tapas town, not a cocktail town. Set expectations accordingly — the free-tapa ritual is the thing here.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

Densest zones: Calle Navas, Calle Elvira / Almireceros (two minutes away), Realejo, and Campo del Príncipe.

  • Bodegas Castañeda — C/ Almireceros 1–3, 3 min. The Granada institution, 1927: wine and house vermouth drawn from wall-mounted barrels, the calicasas barrel blend, jamón carved at the counter. Standing-room scrum, no reservations. You come for the room, not for clever tapas.
  • Los Diamantes — Plaza Nueva 13, right by the hotel. Pescaíto frito since 1942 — the free tapa is a paper cone of fried fish. Loud, tiled, standing.
  • Taberna La Tana — Placeta del Agua 3, Realejo, ~8 min. The serious wine bar: 600+ references, strong on Andalusian small producers. Fills within 20 minutes of opening — arrive at 20:00 sharp. ⚠ Day off unverified; 958 22 52 48.
  • Bar Aliatar — Plaza Aliatar 4, upper Albaicín. A real neighbourhood bar, not a tourist stop: caracoles in spiced broth, chorizo al fuego, choto. Closed Mondays — rules it out on Oct 5.
  • Casa de Vinos La Brujidera — C/ Monjas del Carmen 2, Realejo. Tiny cave-like room walled in bottles; the calm alternative when Castañeda is impossible.
  • Bar Poë — C/ Verónica de la Magdalena 40. Rotating creative free tapas with Portuguese, Angolan and Brazilian leanings. Cash only, evenings.
  • Cocktails — Hanalei (C/ Piedra Santa 22), a properly executed tiki bar with house syrups; or Lemon Rock (C/ Montalbán 6), a restored 17th-century mansion with live music and a roof terrace.

Also worth noting

  • Ronda genuinely lacks a standout cocktail bar. The honest options are Entrevinos for wine instead, or a gin-tonic on the Parador terrace over the Tajo at sunset — scenery, not mixology. Long Play, near the bullring, is a late-night rock bar.
  • Wed Oct 7 is the white-villages day — the Serranía de Ronda wineries are in the On the road section.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

  • Entrevinos — C/ Pozo 2. The town's proper wine bar, list weighted to southern Spain and the Serranía de Ronda, tapas to match. Small, standing room. Closed Sundays. Tue–Sat 12:30–16:30, 20:00–00:30. Arrive at opening.
  • Almocábar — Plaza Ruedo Alameda 5, El Barrio (the atmospheric end of town, by the Almocábar gate). Family-run, seasonal Andalusian: rabo de toro, grilled fish, croquetas, good Sierras de Málaga list, terrace on the plaza. Closed Tuesdays. Very few tables — reserve.
  • Bodega San Francisco — Plaza Ruedo Alameda 27. Sprawling three-room tavern, the local default. Tortitas de camarones, honest raciones. ⚠ Reported closed Thursdays.
  • El Lechuguita — C/ Virgen de los Remedios 35. Tick your order on a paper list, tapas ~€1, packed with rondeños from 21:00. Zero tourism polish.
  • De Locos Tapas — C/ Nueva 93. Six tables, inventive, wine-driven, chef-owner front of house. Good second option if Tragatá is full.

Córdoba

Oct 8 → 10 · 2 nights

Las Casas de la Judería, ~2 min from the Mezquita. Note the local rhythm: many tabernas serve dinner only Mon–Fri and lunch only Sat–Sun.

The single best fit on the whole route. Paco Morales reconstructs the cuisine of Al-Andalus from period sources, each season built around a different century — no tomato, no potato, no chocolate, because none had arrived yet. You spend Oct 9 inside the Mezquita watching Roman columns hold up Moorish arches under a Christian nave, and then eat the same layering.

A three-star at eleven days is the tightest ask on this list. Book this one before anything else.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

  • Bodega Guzmán — C/ Judíos 7, deep in the Judería, minutes from the hotel. The most atmospheric barrel room in the city: Montilla-Moriles fino and amontillado drawn from the cask into thick tumblers, bullfighting shrine on the wall, almost no concession to visitors. Mon–Fri 20:30–23:30; Sat–Sun also 12:00–16:00.
  • Taberna Salinas — C/ Tundidores 3. Open since 1879, patio-centred. The benchmark salmorejo, naranjas picadas con bacalao, rabo de toro, flamenquín. Mon–Fri dinner only; Sat–Sun lunch only. Recommended to reserve.
  • Taberna San Miguel "Casa El Pisto" — Plaza San Miguel 1. An 1880 institution beside the church: pisto, salmorejo, rabo, Montilla by the glass.
  • Bodegas Campos — C/ Lineros 32. A warren of patios and rooms in a 1908 complex, walls signed by decades of visitors. Grander than a taberna but not stiff. Reservation required.
  • Taberna El Número 10 — C/ Romero 10, Judería. Cellar exclusively DO Montilla-Moriles, with tastings — the place to learn the fino/amontillado spectrum. Closed Mondays.
  • Bar Santos — C/ Magistral González Francés 3, literally against the Mezquita wall. The famous towering tortilla española by the wedge on a paper plate, eaten on the steps facing the mosque. Cash only. A ritual, not a meal.

Tapas, wine bars & casual — no booking needed

  • Casa Morales — C/ García de Vinuesa 11, 5 min. Founded 1850, still the same family, fourth generation. Front bar plus a back room of giant clay tinajas. Cold pressed pork belly, chicharrones, tortilla, salmorejo, house manzanilla. The single most atmospheric room in Seville. Closed Sundays.
  • Bodeguita Romero — C/ Harinas 10, 3 min. The definitive montadito de pringá, plus papas aliñás and spinach with chickpeas. Tiny, fiercely local. Closed Sun & Mon → Sat Oct 10 only.
  • El Rinconcillo — C/ Gerona 40, ~12 min. Seville's oldest bar, 1670. Espinacas con garbanzos, jamón, croquetas, bills chalked on the wooden bar. Tapas are served only at the downstairs bar — stand if you want them. ⚠ Three sources give three different closing days; none affects Sat–Mon.
  • Vinería San Telmo — Paseo Catalina de Ribera 4. The wine bar to book: long by-the-glass list including serious sherry, and genuinely creative tapas rather than museum pieces.
  • Espacio Eslava — C/ Eslava 3, San Lorenzo. The best modern tapas cooking in the city and still a bar. Cigarro para Bécquer, slow egg on mushroom cake with honeyed wine, honey-glazed ribs. ⚠ Traditionally closed Sunday evening and Monday — verify, it affects both Oct 11 and 12.
  • Taberna Álvaro Peregil — C/ Mateos Gago 22. A two-metre-wide standing bar pouring sweet vino de naranja since 1904. One drink, one montadito, ten minutes, move on.

Tue Oct 6 — Granada → Ronda, via Antequera & El Torcal

Arte de Cozina

Antequera lunch · reserve

C/ Calzada 27, Antequera · +34 952 84 00 14

Open
Lunch 13:00–16:30 daily, including Tuesday
Price
~€30–40 pp

Exactly your register. Chef Charo Carmona has spent two decades recovering Antequera's own peasant and convent recipes: porra antequerana (and a version with orange), gazpachuelo with shoestring potatoes, goat stew from an old recipe, spinach-and-chickpea stew with a fried egg, bienmesabe for dessert. Notable vermouth and local-wine list, a dining room and a tapas bar both. The only place in Antequera I could verify as reliably open on a Tuesday — Recuerdos Tapas Bodega is Thu–Sun only, so it is shut for you. Reservations recommended, essential for the tasting menu.

The best-organised of the Serranía de Ronda options and the only one with confirmed online booking and published hours. Vineyard, organic garden and olive grove walk, then the cellar, then three wines tasted with Iberian ham, house-baked spelt bread and olive oil — a light meal, not just a sip.

Take the 10:30 and the whole afternoon stays free for the Puerto de las Palomas pass and a long lunch in Zahara. Also offered: a €20 wine-bar tasting with no reservation needed, a €65 "Sunset Experience" with a vineyard picnic, and a bullfighting-art collection visit at €50.

The two alternatives, both requiring a phone call — neither publishes hours or prices, so verify Wednesday directly.

The most beautiful of the three: a restored 16th-century Trinitarian convent in the gorge below the old town, surviving frescoes, vines on the terraces. Small groups only, by arrangement.   • Bodega Joaquín Fernández — Paraje Los Frontones, ~3 km south of Ronda. Sat Oct 10 — Córdoba → Seville, via Carmona

A 15th-century Mudéjar olive mill: vine-shaded central patio, thick stone rooms, and a terrace with the same view over the Corbones plain as the Parador, for considerably less money. Lamb chops, pluma ibérica, artisan anchovies, croquetas, house desserts.

Recommended over the Parador. The Parador terrace is one of the great lunch views in Andalusia — a Moorish alcázar on the escarpment — but the food is Parador-standard rather than exciting. Same landscape here, far more character.

molinodelaromera.es·Parador de Carmona

The other Carmona option — Casa Curro Montoya, C/ Santa María de Gracia 13 (+34 657 90 36 29). Small, owner-run, bullfighting décor, a tiny terrace in the historic centre. Artichokes, seafood, jamón, seasonal plates — the most personal cooking in town, priced above what the room suggests. Saturday 13:30–16:00, closed Tuesdays. No view.

Carmona itself: the Roman necropolis, the Alcázar de la Puerta de Sevilla (Carthaginian → Roman → Moorish in one gate-fortress), and the Puerta de Córdoba at the far end of the old town. Then on toward Santa Justa to drop the car before entering Seville.
